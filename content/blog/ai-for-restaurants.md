Ask any restaurant manager when the phone rings most and they will tell you: right in the middle of the rush. Someone wants to know if you're open on Monday. Someone else wants to book for eight on Saturday. A third person is calling to ask if you have parking. Meanwhile the host is seating a two-top, the bar is three deep and nobody can pick up.

AI is not going to cook or run food. But it can take a surprising amount of repetitive work off your front-of-house team during service and off your desk after close. This playbook covers the workflows that tend to help independent restaurants, small groups and catering operations the most.

## How can a restaurant use AI without losing the personal touch?

**Use AI for the repetitive, low-stakes tasks like routine phone questions, reservation changes, catering follow-up and invoice entry, and keep people on anything involving guests' experience, allergies, complaints or money.** Your staff get more time for the guests actually in the building.

| Area | Good for AI | Keep with people |
|---|---|---|
| Phones | Hours, directions, parking, menu basics, reservation changes | Complaints, allergy questions, VIPs |
| Reservations | Booking, confirming, waitlist texts | Large parties, special occasions, comps |
| Catering and events | Instant reply, collecting details, follow-up | Menus, pricing, final proposals |
| Reviews | Drafting replies, routing negative feedback | Approving replies, making things right |
| Back office | Invoice entry, price change alerts, weekly reports | Ordering decisions, payroll approval |

## How can AI answer restaurant phone calls?

**An AI phone assistant answers routine calls instantly, such as hours, location, parking, menu questions and reservations, and sends takeout callers a link to your online ordering.** It can answer when the host is slammed or the restaurant is closed, and transfer to a person whenever a caller needs one.

Most restaurant calls fall into a handful of buckets:

- "Are you open tonight? Until what time?"
- "Do you take reservations? Can I book for Friday?"
- "Do you have vegetarian or gluten-free options?"
- "Is there parking?" or "Do you have outdoor seating?"
- "Can I place a pickup order?"
- "Do you do private events or catering?"

An [AI receptionist](/services/ai-receptionist) answers all of these from information you approve: your hours, holiday schedule, menu, policies and FAQs. For pickup orders, most restaurants have the AI text a link to their online ordering, whether that's Toast, Square or another platform, instead of taking orders by voice. That's usually faster and more accurate, and the order goes straight to the kitchen.

### A firm rule on allergies

If a caller asks "Is the pad thai safe for a peanut allergy?", the AI should not guess. It can say which menu items you list as containing common allergens, if you provide that information, and then offer to connect the caller to a manager or chef. Kitchens change recipes and suppliers, and cross-contact is not something to leave to software. Make this a handoff rule, not a judgment call.

## How can AI help with reservations and waitlists?

**AI can book, confirm and change reservations by phone or text, send reminders and run a text-based waitlist, connecting to your reservation platform where it supports integration.** The goal is fewer no-shows and fewer phone calls during service.

Practical uses:

- **Bookings by phone:** The AI checks availability and books, or texts a booking link if your system has no integration.
- **Confirmations and reminders:** A confirmation at booking and a reminder the day before, with an easy way to cancel or change. Freed-up tables can go to the waitlist.
- **Large parties:** Requests above a size you choose go to a manager with all the details collected.
- **Special occasions:** The AI notes birthdays and anniversaries so your team can plan a touch.

Platforms like OpenTable, Resy and Tock already handle much of this for online bookings. AI fills the gap on the phone and in text conversations. For more on reminders, see [reducing no-shows with AI reminders](/blog/reduce-no-shows-with-ai-reminders).

## How should restaurants handle catering and private event leads?

**Reply to every catering and private event inquiry within minutes, collect the details your events team needs and follow up until the lead books or says no.** These are often the highest-value inquiries a restaurant gets, and they often sit in an inbox for days.

A good intake collects:

- Date, time and guest count
- Event type (corporate lunch, rehearsal dinner, birthday, holiday party)
- Pickup, delivery, full service or on-site private dining
- Budget range and dietary needs
- Contact details and decision timeline

Then a simple follow-up sequence:

1. **Within minutes:** Thank them, confirm the details and share your catering or private dining menu.
2. **Same day:** Your events manager sends a proposal (AI can draft it from your packages).
3. **Day 3:** A friendly check-in asking if they have questions.
4. **Day 7:** A reminder about date availability, if it's genuinely filling up.
5. **After the event:** A thank-you, a review request and a note to reach out for the next one.

Corporate clients who order lunch once may well order again. A quarterly check-in to past catering customers can bring steady repeat business. Our [lead follow-up service](/services/lead-follow-up) can set this up end to end.

## How can AI help with restaurant reviews?

**AI drafts personalized replies to reviews in your restaurant's voice, flags negative reviews for a manager right away and sends review requests to happy guests on Google.** A manager approves every reply before it is posted.

A sample AI-drafted reply to a mixed review:

> Thanks so much for coming in for your anniversary, Priya, and for the kind words about the short rib. You're right that the wait for your table was too long on Saturday, and we're sorry it took the shine off the start of your night. We've adjusted how we pace reservations on weekends. We'd love to have you back. Ask for me, Tony, next time.

A few rules of the road:

- **Ask for Google reviews, not Yelp.** Yelp discourages businesses from asking for reviews, so keep requests focused on your Google Business Profile.
- **Never offer incentives for reviews.** Google and Yelp both prohibit it; check each platform's current policy.
- **Reply to negative reviews personally.** AI can draft, but a human should approve and, ideally, reach out offline.
- **Texting needs consent.** Only text guests who opted in, and register your texting number under A2P 10DLC.

## What back-of-house admin can AI automate?

**AI can enter vendor invoices, flag price changes, build weekly sales and labor reports and help draft schedules.** These are the tasks that keep owners at the restaurant long after close.

### Vendor invoice entry and price alerts

Restaurants receive a stack of invoices every week from broadline distributors, produce companies, meat purveyors and beverage suppliers. AI can read each invoice, whether it's a PDF or a photo, enter it into your accounting or inventory system and flag when a price jumps. For example: "Chicken thighs went up 14% from last week's invoice." That alert alone can prompt a menu price review or a call to a second vendor. See [how to automate data entry and paperwork](/blog/automate-data-entry-and-paperwork) for how this works.

### Weekly prime cost and sales reports

Your POS has sales data, your scheduling tool has labor and your invoices have food and beverage costs. AI can combine them into a one-page Monday morning report: sales vs. last week, labor as a percentage of sales, food cost trends and any red flags. Your bookkeeper still closes the books, but you see the trend while you can still act on it.

### Scheduling help

Using past covers, reservations on the books and local events, AI can suggest a staffing level for each shift. Your manager builds the final schedule in a tool like 7shifts. AI can also handle shift swap requests by checking who is available and qualified.

### An illustrative time-savings example

This is a hypothetical example, not a client result. Suppose a single-location restaurant's general manager spends about 4 hours a week entering invoices, 2 hours building a weekly cost report and 3 hours on catering inquiries and review replies. That's 9 hours a week. If AI cuts that in half, the GM gets back 4.5 hours a week, roughly 230 hours a year, for the floor, training and guests. ## What should restaurant owners watch out for?

**Keep AI answers grounded in information you approve, route allergies and complaints to people and keep your information current.** An AI that confidently gives last month's hours is worse than voicemail.

- Update holiday hours, menu changes and special events before they happen.
- Always offer a way to reach a human.
- Never let AI comp meals, promise refunds or discuss allergens beyond your approved list.
- Review call summaries and drafts weekly for the first month.
- Start with one workflow, usually the phones or invoice entry, and expand.
