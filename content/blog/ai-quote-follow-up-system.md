You drove out, measured, priced it, wrote it up, and sent the quote. Then nothing. No yes, no no. Two weeks later you find out the customer went with someone else, or worse, never decided and is still living with the problem.

Plenty of small businesses don't lose quoted jobs on price. They lose them to silence. This post walks through a simple AI quote follow-up system you can run on every estimate, including a five-touch sequence with sample copy you can adapt today.

## Why do so many quoted jobs never close?

**Because nobody follows up consistently.** The estimate goes out, the customer gets busy, and your team is too buried in today's work to chase last week's quotes.

Think about what happens on the customer's side. They asked three companies for a price. They got your quote on a Tuesday night. They meant to talk to their spouse about it. They had a question about the warranty but didn't want to call. Then life happened.

The company that checks in politely, answers the question, and makes it easy to say yes often wins. Often that isn't the cheapest company. It's the one that showed up again.

Common reasons quotes stall:

- The customer had a question and didn't want to call to ask it
- They needed to talk to a spouse, partner, landlord, or manager
- They were waiting on money, a tax refund, or a slower season
- They forgot, or your quote was buried in their inbox
- They're comparing options and nobody has helped them decide

Every one of those is solvable with a well-timed, helpful message. None of them are solved by waiting.

## What is an AI quote follow-up system?

**It's an automated sequence that starts the moment you send an estimate and keeps a polite, personal conversation going until the customer books, declines, or asks you to stop.** AI writes and personalizes the messages, handles simple replies, and hands real conversations to your team.

It's a specific kind of [lead nurturing](/glossary/lead-nurturing), and it usually has four parts:

1. **A trigger.** A quote is marked "sent" in your [CRM](/glossary/crm) or field service software.
2. **A sequence.** A set of texts and emails on a schedule, each with a different purpose.
3. **Stop rules.** Any reply, booking, or "no thanks" ends the sequence immediately.
4. **A handoff.** Questions and objections go to a person, with the quote details attached.

The AI piece matters because it lets each message reference the actual job ("the 2-ton condenser replacement" rather than "your recent quote"), answer basic questions after hours, and summarize the conversation so whoever picks it up isn't starting cold.

## What does a 5-touch quote follow-up sequence look like?

**Five touches over about three weeks, alternating text and email, each with a different job to do.** Here's a sequence that works for most home service, trade, and project-based businesses.

| Touch | Timing | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Same day, 1–2 hours after the quote | Text | Confirm they got it and invite questions |
| 2 | Day 2 | Email | Resend the quote with a plain-English summary |
| 3 | Day 5 | Text | Address the most common hesitation |
| 4 | Day 10 | Email | Offer options or help them decide |
| 5 | Day 21 | Text | Polite close-the-loop message |

Adjust the timing to your sales cycle. An emergency repair quote might compress all five touches into a week. A $40,000 remodel might stretch to six weeks.

### Sample copy for each touch

These examples use an HVAC replacement, but the structure works for roofing, painting, landscaping, concrete, cleaning contracts, or any quoted service. Replace the bracketed details with real ones pulled from the quote.

**Touch 1: Same-day text**

> Hi [First name], it's [Rep name] from [Company]. Just sent over the quote for the [new heat pump system]. Any questions at all, just reply here. Happy to walk through it.

Short, human, and it opens a channel. Many customers will reply right here with the question that was going to stall them.

**Touch 2: Day 2 email**

> Subject: Your [heat pump] quote, in plain English
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> Here's a quick summary of what we quoted so it's easy to compare:
>
> - What's included: [equipment, install, removal of old unit, permit]
> - Warranty: [10-year parts, 2-year labor]
> - Timeline: [We can usually install within 5–7 business days of approval]
> - Total: [$X], with [financing options if you offer them]
>
> The full quote is attached. If anything is unclear, or you're comparing other bids and want help understanding the differences, just reply.
>
> [Rep name]

The "comparing other bids" line is deliberate. It invites the conversation instead of pretending competitors don't exist.

**Touch 3: Day 5 text**

> Hi [First name], [Rep name] again. A lot of folks ask about [financing / the install timeline / what happens to the old unit] at this point. Want me to send details on that?

Pick the question your customers ask most often. If you don't know it, ask your best salesperson. They'll tell you in five seconds.

**Touch 4: Day 10 email**

> Subject: A couple of options for your [project]
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> If the full quote isn't quite right, we have a couple of ways to adjust it:
>
> - [Option A: a lower-cost version, e.g. a different efficiency tier]
> - [Option B: phasing the work, e.g. doing the front of the house now and the rest in spring]
>
> No pressure either way. I just want to make sure you have what you need to decide.
>
> [Rep name]

This touch rescues price-sensitive customers who would otherwise disappear without telling you the budget was the problem.

**Touch 5: Day 21 text**

> Hi [First name], I don't want to keep bugging you about the [project] quote. Should I keep it open, or close it out for now? Either answer is totally fine.

The close-the-loop message often draws replies when earlier touches didn't, because it gives people permission to say no. A clear "not now" is valuable too: it tells you to move them to a seasonal check-in list rather than marking them lost forever.

## What rules keep quote follow-up from being annoying?

**Stop the moment the customer engages, respect their channel preferences, and make every message useful.** Automation without stop rules is how businesses end up texting "just checking in!" to someone who signed yesterday.

Non-negotiable rules:

- **Stop on any reply.** A human (or the AI for simple questions) takes over from there.
- **Stop on status change.** If the quote is marked won, lost, or scheduled in your system, the sequence ends.
- **Respect opt-outs.** "Stop," "unsubscribe," or "please don't text me" ends texting for good.
- **Send at reasonable hours.** No texts before 8 a.m. or after 8 p.m. in the customer's time zone (some states set their own quiet hours, so check yours).
- **One sequence at a time.** A customer with two open quotes shouldn't get two parallel streams of messages.

On the compliance side, keep it simple: only text people who've given you their number for that purpose and agreed to receive texts, include opt-out language, and make sure your business texting number is registered for A2P 10DLC so carriers don't block your messages. TCPA rules apply to marketing texts, so if you're unsure, get a quick review from your attorney before you launch.

## How does this work with the software you already use?

**The follow-up system connects to wherever your quotes already live, so nobody has to remember to start or stop it.** That's the part most DIY attempts get wrong.

Here's the before and after for a typical small contractor:

**Before:**
1. Owner sends the quote from Jobber or a PDF on Sunday night.
2. Plans to call on Thursday. Doesn't.
3. Customer texts a question to the owner's cell. It gets buried.
4. Quote quietly expires. Nobody knows why it was lost.

**After:**
1. Quote marked "sent" in your software triggers the sequence automatically.
2. Customer replies to Touch 1 with a warranty question; AI answers from your approved warranty info and flags the rep.
3. Customer approves on Day 6; the system marks the quote won and stops all messages.
4. A weekly report shows every open quote, where it is in the sequence, and which ones replied.

Most popular field service and CRM tools (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, HubSpot, and others) either include basic follow-up features or connect through an [API integration](/glossary/api-integration). The work is in making quote status flow both ways, writing messages that sound like you, and routing replies to the right person.
