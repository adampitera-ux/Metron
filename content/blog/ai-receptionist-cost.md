If you're pricing out an AI receptionist, you've probably seen numbers from $25 a month to well over $1,000. That spread is real, and it exists because "AI receptionist" covers very different things: a self-serve app you set up yourself, a done-for-you system someone builds and maintains around your business, and everything in between. This guide breaks down what you actually pay for, the costs most pricing pages leave out, and how to decide whether it's worth it for your business.

## How much does an AI receptionist cost in 2026?

**Self-serve AI receptionist apps commonly cost about $25 to $300 a month in 2026, depending on call volume and features, while done-for-you setups cost more because they include design, integration and ongoing management.** The price you pay depends mostly on three things: how many calls you get, whether the AI books appointments directly into your calendar, and who does the setup work.

Here's how the main options compare on cost structure:

| Option | Typical cost structure | What you get | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-serve AI receptionist app | Flat monthly plan, per call or per minute; entry plans often under $100/month | Answers calls, takes messages, sometimes books appointments; you configure it | Owners comfortable setting up software themselves |
| Done-for-you AI receptionist | Setup fee plus monthly management | Custom call flows, calendar and CRM integration, missed-call text-back, ongoing tuning | Businesses that want it working without learning a new tool |
| Live answering service | Per minute or per call, rising with volume | Human agents take messages, sometimes book appointments | Businesses that need a human voice on every call |
| In-house receptionist | Salary plus payroll taxes, benefits and time off | A person who also handles walk-ins and other office work | Busy offices with in-person needs |

For context, a full-time receptionist costs far more than any software once you add payroll taxes, benefits, training and coverage for breaks and vacations. That doesn't make software automatically better. A person handles judgment calls and in-person work an AI can't. But for answering the phone 24/7, the cost difference is large.

## What do the pricing models mean?

**Most AI receptionist pricing is per call, per minute or a flat monthly fee, and the right model depends on how many calls you get and how long they last.**

- **Per call:** You pay for each answered call. Predictable if your calls are long, expensive if you get lots of quick calls and spam.
- **Per minute:** You pay for talk time. Cheap for short calls, but costs climb in busy months, such as storm season for roofers or the first heat wave for HVAC.
- **Flat monthly:** A set price for a set amount of usage, sometimes unlimited. Easiest to budget, but check what happens when you go over.

Ask any provider for your estimated monthly cost at both your normal and your busiest call volume. If the answer in your busiest month makes you wince, keep looking.

## What hidden costs do most AI receptionist pricing pages leave out?

**The biggest costs that don't show up on pricing pages are setup time, call-flow design, integrations, compliance and ongoing tuning.** These are where most self-serve setups stall.

1. **Call-flow design.** Someone has to decide what the AI asks, what it says about pricing, how it handles emergencies and when it transfers to a person. Done well, this takes real thought about your business. Done badly, callers get frustrated and hang up.
2. **Calendar and CRM integration.** An AI that only takes messages saves you less than one that books jobs directly. Connecting it to your scheduling or field-service software, such as Jobber, Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan, is often an add-on or a do-it-yourself project.
3. **Phone setup.** Forwarding your existing number, porting numbers and setting rules for business hours, overflow and after-hours calls all take time to get right.
4. **Testing and tuning.** The first version is never the final version. Real callers ask things you didn't predict, and the system needs adjustment in the first few weeks.
5. **Compliance.** Some states require every party's consent to record a call, and follow-up texts must follow the TCPA and carrier rules. More on this in our guide to [texting rules for AI follow-up](/blog/tcpa-texting-rules-for-small-business).
6. **Your time.** If you're the one configuring and fixing the system, your hours are part of the cost.

This is the gap between a $50 app and a system that actually books jobs. Some owners love building it themselves. Many sign up, set it up halfway, and go back to voicemail.

## How do you calculate whether an AI receptionist pays for itself?

**Multiply the calls you miss each month by the share that would have become jobs and your average job value, then compare that monthly revenue at risk to the cost.** If the AI recovers even a few jobs a month, it usually covers its cost.

Here's an illustrative example with made-up numbers:

- Missed calls per month: **40**
- Share that would have booked: **25%** → 10 jobs
- Average job value: **$300**
- Revenue at risk: 10 × $300 = **$3,000 a month**

If an AI receptionist captures even half of those, that's about $1,500 a month in recovered work, against a monthly cost that's a fraction of that. ## What should you ask before buying an AI receptionist?

**Ask how it books jobs, how it handles emergencies, what happens when it's unsure, how it handles compliance, and what you'll pay in your busiest month.** These questions separate tools that work from tools that frustrate callers.

- Can it book directly into my calendar or field-service software, or does it only take messages?
- How does it recognize and route emergencies to a person?
- What does it do when it doesn't know the answer?
- Can callers reach a human at any point?
- Does it text back missed calls automatically?
- How does it handle call recording consent and text opt-outs?
- Who writes and updates the call scripts, and how quickly can changes be made?
- What will I pay at my busiest call volume?
- Do I own my call data and recordings?

## DIY app or done-for-you: which should you choose?

**Choose a self-serve app if you enjoy configuring software and have simple call needs; choose done-for-you if you want it working well without becoming the system administrator.** Both are valid. The wrong choice is buying a tool you never finish setting up.

Done-for-you makes the most sense when:

- You want calls booked into your existing software, not just messages taken.
- You get calls that need different handling, like emergencies, quotes and existing customers.
- You'd rather spend your time running jobs than tuning call flows.
