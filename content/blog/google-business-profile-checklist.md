For most local service businesses, your Google Business Profile is more important than your website when it comes to getting found. It's what shows up in the map pack when someone searches "plumber near me," and it's increasingly what Google's AI Overviews pull from. This checklist covers everything you control, in the order that matters.

## How does Google rank Google Business Profiles?

**Google ranks local results on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence.** Distance is where the searcher is, which you can't control. Relevance and prominence are where you win.

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone searched for. Categories, services and descriptions drive this.
- **Distance:** how close you are to the searcher or the location in their search.
- **Prominence:** how well known and trusted your business is, through reviews, links, mentions and overall web presence.

## The Google Business Profile checklist

### 1. Set up the basics correctly

**Use your real business name, the right address setup and a phone number that matches your website.**

- Business name exactly as it appears on your signage and paperwork. No added keywords.
- If customers come to you, show your address. If you go to customers, set up as a service-area business and list the cities or areas you serve.
- A local phone number that matches your website and other listings.
- Your website URL, ideally pointing to your home page or a location page.
- Accurate business hours, plus holiday hours.

### 2. Choose the right categories

**Your primary category is one of the most important ranking settings, so pick the most specific one that matches your main service.**

- Primary: the most specific match, like "Plumber," "HVAC contractor" or "Roofing contractor."
- Secondary: add others that genuinely apply, like "Water heater installation service."
- Look at which categories the top-ranking competitors in your area use.

### 3. List every service

**Add every service you offer with a short description, because services help Google match you to more searches.**

- Add your core services and the specific jobs people search for, like "drain cleaning" or "AC repair."
- Write a sentence or two for each.
- Make sure your website has pages for your main services too.

### 4. Write a clear business description

**Explain what you do, who you serve and where, in plain language.** You have 750 characters. Use them on what customers care about: services, service area, experience and what makes you different. Don't stuff keywords.

### 5. Add real photos regularly

**Upload real photos of your team, trucks, work and results, and keep adding new ones.** Real photos build trust and keep your profile active. Avoid stock images.

- Logo and cover photo.
- Team and trucks.
- Before-and-after job photos.
- Your shop or office, if customers visit.

### 6. Get steady, recent reviews

**Ask every happy customer for a review right after the job, and keep the reviews coming steadily.** Reviews are one of the strongest prominence signals, and recent reviews tell customers and Google you're active.

- Send a review request by text right after the job, with a direct link.
- Make it easy: one tap to the review form.
- Never buy reviews or offer incentives for positive reviews, which violates Google's policies.

Automating this is the single highest-impact change for most businesses. See [how to get more Google reviews automatically](/blog/get-more-google-reviews-automatically).

### 7. Respond to every review

**Reply to every review, good and bad, quickly and professionally.** It shows customers you care and gives you a chance to address problems. Keep negative-review replies calm, short and focused on making it right.

### 8. Post updates

**Post updates, offers or recent projects regularly so your profile stays fresh.** Posts are a simple way to show you're active.

### 9. Answer common questions

**Add answers to the questions customers ask most, either in your profile or on your website.** Clear answers help customers and help AI search tools describe your business accurately.

### 10. Keep your info consistent everywhere

**Make sure your business name, address and phone (NAP) match exactly across your website, directories and social profiles.** Inconsistent listings confuse Google and customers.

- Check Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook, BBB and industry directories.
- Update old addresses and phone numbers.
- Use the same format everywhere.

### 11. Connect your website

**Your website should reinforce your profile with the same NAP, service pages and local information.** Add structured data so search engines understand your business. See [schema markup for small business](/blog/llms-txt-schema-markup-for-small-business).

### 12. Track what's working

**Check your profile's performance each month: calls, website clicks, direction requests and searches.** Use call tracking or UTM links so you know which leads come from your profile.

## What mistakes hurt Google Business Profile rankings?

**The most common mistakes are keyword-stuffed names, wrong categories, inconsistent info, ignored reviews and fake addresses.**

- Adding keywords or cities to your business name.
- Using a virtual office or PO box as your address.
- Choosing a broad primary category.
- Letting reviews go unanswered.
- Going months without new reviews or photos.
- Different phone numbers on different sites.
