Ask ChatGPT "who's the best family dentist in [your town]?" right now. Then ask Perplexity. Then Google it and look at the AI Overview. Are you in any of those answers?

If not, this guide is for you. It's a practical, honest playbook for getting your business recommended by the major AI tools, with no tricks and no promises anyone can't keep.

## Can you actually get your business recommended by ChatGPT?

**You can't force it, but you can make your business much easier for AI tools to find, verify and describe, which improves your odds.** Anyone promising guaranteed placement in organic AI answers is overselling.

AI tools build recommendations by summarizing what they know or find about businesses. Your job is to make sure that what they find is:

1. **Findable:** your site and listings can be accessed and indexed.
2. **Verifiable:** the same facts show up consistently across many trustworthy sources.
3. **Describable:** your content clearly states what you do, where and for whom.

Everything in this guide serves one of those three.

## How do ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews find businesses?

**They use a mix of what the model learned in training and what it retrieves from the web or a search index when answering.** Each tool works a little differently, and none publishes a full selection formula.

| Tool | How it generally gets current information | What that means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** | Can search the web when answering and cite sources; otherwise relies on training data | Be crawlable and well-represented on the web; training data alone may be thin or outdated for small businesses |
| **Perplexity** | Built around live web search with visible source citations | Pages that clearly answer the question are more likely to be cited |
| **Google AI Overviews** | Google says it draws on its regular search systems and index | Standard SEO and a strong Google Business Profile carry over directly |
| **Gemini / Copilot** | Can draw on Google and Bing search respectively | Being well-indexed in both Google and Bing helps |

The common thread: **if search engines and AI crawlers can't access your site, or your information is inconsistent, every tool has less to work with.**

## What's the step-by-step playbook to get recommended?

**Work through seven steps in order: audit, fix your facts, define your business, answer real questions, earn reviews, earn mentions, and make sure you're crawlable.** Many small businesses can complete the first pass in a month or two.

### Step 1: Audit what AI tools say about you today

Pick 10 questions your customers actually ask. Mix in:

- Recommendation questions: "best [service] in [city]," "who does [specific job] near [neighborhood]"
- Problem questions: "why is my furnace blowing cold air," "is a chipped tooth an emergency"
- Brand questions: "is [your business name] reputable," "what does [your business] charge for [service]"

Run them through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google. Record who gets named, which sources are cited and anything wrong about you. Save this as your baseline.

### Step 2: Fix your business facts everywhere

Inconsistent details make it harder for any system to be confident about who you are. Make these identical everywhere:

- Business name (exactly the same spelling)
- Address or service area
- Phone number
- Hours
- Primary category and core services
- Website URL

Start with Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Facebook and the top directories in your industry. Then check your own website's header, footer and contact page.

### Step 3: Write a page that clearly defines your business

AI tools need plain statements they can summarize. Your "About" or home page should say, in simple sentences:

- What you do ("We're a residential plumbing company specializing in water heaters and repiping")
- Where ("serving Tempe, Mesa and Chandler")
- Who you serve best ("homeowners and small property managers")
- Credentials and history ("licensed, insured, family-owned since 2009")
- What makes you different, stated specifically

This is the heart of [entity SEO](/glossary/entity-seo): making your business a well-defined thing that machines can recognize and describe.

### Step 4: Answer real customer questions on your site

Build or rewrite service pages using answer-first structure: the question as a heading, a direct 1–3 sentence answer, then detail. Cover costs (honest ranges are fine), timelines, "do I need this," and "repair or replace" questions.

Our [AEO guide](/blog/what-is-aeo-answer-engine-optimization) walks through this format with examples.

### Step 5: Earn a steady stream of detailed reviews

Reviews are one of the most public, crawlable signals about a local business. What helps most:

- **Consistency:** reviews coming in every week, not a burst once a year.
- **Detail:** reviews that mention the specific service and location.
- **Responses:** thoughtful replies from you, which add context.

The hard part is remembering to ask. [Review automation](/services/review-automation) sends a request by text after every completed job, so it happens without anyone on your team chasing it. Never buy or fake reviews.

### Step 6: Earn mentions on other trusted sites

Generative tools tend to synthesize what many sources say. Places worth getting listed or mentioned include:

- Manufacturer or supplier "find a dealer/installer" pages
- Industry associations and licensing boards
- Local chamber of commerce and business associations
- Local news (offer expert comments on seasonal topics)
- Community sponsorships with a link back to you

### Step 7: Make sure you're technically accessible

- Confirm your important pages are indexed in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Check your robots.txt isn't accidentally blocking search engines or AI search crawlers you want to reach you.
- Add [schema markup](/glossary/schema-markup) for your business type and FAQs.
- Consider an [llms.txt](/glossary/llms-txt) file, understanding it's an emerging, proposed convention rather than an official standard.

Our [technical AEO checklist](/blog/llms-txt-schema-markup-for-small-business) covers all of this in detail.

## Should you let AI crawlers access your website?

**If you want AI tools to recommend you, generally yes: at minimum, allow crawlers used for AI search features.** Blocking them means those tools have less first-hand information about you.

Some AI companies use different crawlers for different purposes. For example, OpenAI documents separate user agents for model training (GPTBot) and for search features (OAI-SearchBot). Google offers a "Google-Extended" control for whether your content is used for its Gemini models, separate from regular Google Search crawling. That lets you make deliberate choices.

The mistake to avoid is accidentally blocking everything because a security plugin, CDN setting or website builder turned on a default. Check your robots.txt and firewall settings on purpose.

## What mistakes stop small businesses from being recommended?

**Common problems include inconsistent information, vague websites, few recent reviews and an unanswered phone.** Here's a quick diagnostic.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| AI lists wrong hours or old phone number | Outdated directory listings | Update listings at the source (Step 2) |
| AI describes you vaguely or not at all | Website doesn't clearly state what you do | Write a defining page (Step 3) |
| Competitors named, you're not | Fewer reviews and mentions | Steps 5 and 6 |
| You're mentioned but leads don't convert | Calls go to voicemail or slow follow-up | [AI receptionist](/services/ai-receptionist) and fast lead response |
| Nothing from your site is ever cited | Crawl or indexing issues, or no answer-style content | Steps 4 and 7 |

That fourth row deserves attention. When an AI tool recommends three businesses, the customer often calls all three. Whoever answers first often wins. If you're missing calls today, more AI visibility just means more missed opportunities. ## How do you measure whether it's working?

**Combine monthly manual checks, analytics referral data and simply asking new customers how they found you.** No single metric captures AI visibility yet.

- **Re-run your baseline questions monthly** and note changes in who's named and what's said.
- **Check analytics for referrals** from AI tools; some, like ChatGPT and Perplexity, can show up as referral sources when users click links, though not every visit is labeled clearly.
- **Ask on every call or form:** "How did you hear about us?" Add "ChatGPT / AI assistant" as an option.
- **Watch Search Console** for growth in question-style queries.

## Key takeaways

- **No one can guarantee AI recommendations,** but you can make your business findable, verifiable and describable.
- **Consistent business facts across the web** are an often overlooked fix.
- **A clear "who we are" page and answer-first service pages** give AI tools something accurate to summarize.
- **Detailed, steady reviews and third-party mentions** build the corroboration AI tools look for.
- **Don't accidentally block** the crawlers and search engines you want to reach you.
- **Be ready to answer every call,** because AI recommendations send customers shopping fast.
