Every business gets a bad review eventually. What matters is how you respond, because future customers read your reply as much as the review itself. A calm, helpful response can actually build trust. A defensive one can cost you jobs for years. Here's a simple framework and templates you can adapt.

## How should a small business respond to a negative review?

**Thank the reviewer, acknowledge the specific issue, take responsibility where it's fair, offer to resolve it offline with a direct contact, and keep it short.** Write for the next customer reading, not to win an argument.

### The 5-step framework

1. **Thank them.** "Thanks for taking the time to share this."
2. **Acknowledge the specific issue.** Show you actually read it: "I'm sorry the technician arrived two hours late."
3. **Take responsibility where it's fair.** "That's not the experience we want anyone to have."
4. **Move it offline.** "Please call me directly at [number] so I can make this right."
5. **Sign it.** Use your name and role. It shows a real person cares.

Keep replies to three to five sentences. Long explanations read like excuses.

## 8 templates for negative review responses

Replace [brackets] with your details. Edit each one so it fits the actual review; copy-paste replies are obvious.

**1. Service didn't meet expectations**
> Hi [Name], thank you for the feedback. I'm sorry the [service] didn't meet your expectations. That's not our standard. I'd like to understand what happened and make it right. Please call me at [phone]. — [Your name], [Role]

**2. Late arrival or scheduling problem**
> Hi [Name], I'm sorry we arrived later than promised. We know your time matters. We're tightening how we schedule and communicate delays. I'd like to make this up to you; please reach me at [phone]. — [Your name]

**3. Price complaint**
> Hi [Name], thanks for sharing this. I'm sorry the final price was a surprise. We aim to be clear about costs before work starts. I'd like to walk through the invoice with you. Please call me at [phone]. — [Your name]

**4. Rude or unprofessional staff**
> Hi [Name], I'm sorry for how you were treated. That's not acceptable and not who we want to be. I'm addressing it with our team. I'd appreciate the chance to talk with you directly at [phone]. — [Your name], Owner

**5. Work quality issue**
> Hi [Name], thank you for letting us know. I'm sorry the [work] isn't holding up the way it should. We stand behind our work, and I'd like to send someone out to take a look. Please call me at [phone]. — [Your name]

**6. Long wait or no response**
> Hi [Name], I'm sorry we were slow to get back to you. You deserved a faster response. We've changed how we handle incoming calls and messages. If you're still open to it, I'd love to help; call me at [phone]. — [Your name]

**7. Review you can't match to a customer**
> Hi [Name], thanks for your review. We take feedback seriously, but we can't find a record of your visit. Please contact me at [phone] so I can look into this and help. — [Your name]

**8. Mixed review (some good, some bad)**
> Hi [Name], thanks for the kind words about [positive point], and I'm sorry about [issue]. We're working on it. I'd love to hear more about how we can improve; reach me at [phone] anytime. — [Your name]

## What should you never say in a review response?

**Never argue, blame the customer, share private details, or offer something in exchange for removing the review.**

- Don't get defensive or sarcastic, even if the review is unfair.
- Don't share the customer's personal information, job details or invoice amounts.
- Don't accuse them of lying in public.
- Don't offer discounts or refunds in exchange for deleting or changing a review.
- Don't copy and paste the same reply to every review.

## When should you flag a review instead?

**Flag reviews that clearly break Google's policies, such as spam, fake reviews, conflicts of interest, off-topic content or offensive language.** In your Google Business Profile, select the review and report it. Google won't remove a review just because it's negative.

## How do you prevent negative reviews from dominating your profile?

**Ask every happy customer for a review, every time.** Most satisfied customers never leave one unless asked, so a few unhappy reviews can outweigh hundreds of happy customers. A simple text with a direct link right after each job changes that balance.
