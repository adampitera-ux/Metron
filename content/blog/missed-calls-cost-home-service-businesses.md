An unanswered call is often a customer who was ready to spend money and is now dialing your competitor. Most home service owners know they miss calls. Few know what those calls are actually worth. This post gives you a simple formula, an illustrative worked example, and the fixes that recover the most revenue for the least effort.

## How much does a missed call cost a home service business?

**A missed call costs you the job you would have booked, multiplied by how often you'd have booked it, plus the future work that customer would have brought.** For a service business with a few hundred dollars per ticket, even a handful of missed job calls a week adds up to thousands of dollars a month.

There's no universal number, because it depends on your trade, your average ticket, and your booking rate. That's why the formula matters more than any headline statistic.

## What is the formula for missed-call revenue loss?

**Lost revenue = missed job calls × booking rate × average ticket.** Then add a lifetime value adjustment for repeat customers and referrals.

Break it down:

1. **Missed job calls.** Not every missed call is a job. Exclude spam, vendors, and existing customers who'd call back anyway. Your phone system's missed-call report is the starting point.
2. **Booking rate.** Of the calls your team does answer, what share become booked jobs? Use your real number if you have it.
3. **Average ticket.** Average revenue per first job, not your biggest jobs.
4. **Lifetime value (optional but important).** A new customer may come back for maintenance, replacements, or refer neighbors. Even a conservative multiplier changes the picture.

You can plug your own numbers into our [missed-call revenue calculator](/tools/missed-call-revenue-calculator).

### An illustrative example

These numbers are made up to show the math. Use yours.

Imagine a plumbing company that:

- Misses 6 job-related calls a week
- Books 50% of the calls it answers
- Has a $400 average first-job ticket

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Missed job calls per month | 6 × 4.3 weeks | ~26 calls |
| Jobs that would have booked | 26 × 50% | ~13 jobs |
| First-job revenue lost | 13 × $400 | ~$5,200/month |
| Annualized | $5,200 × 12 | ~$62,400/year |

That's before counting a single repeat visit or referral. If even a portion of those customers would have joined a maintenance plan or called again for a water heater replacement, the real number is higher.

Now flip it: if you recovered half of those missed jobs, that's roughly $2,600 a month in this example, from calls you were already paying to generate.

## Why do home service businesses miss so many calls?

**Because the people who answer the phone are usually the same people doing the work.** Techs are under a sink or on a roof, the office is on another line, and calls spike exactly when everyone is busiest.

The most common causes:

- **After-hours and weekends.** Emergencies don't wait for business hours, and a lot of homeowners call in the evening after work.
- **Peak-season spikes.** The first heat wave or cold snap floods the phone lines for days.
- **Lunch breaks and shift gaps.** Short windows add up.
- **Small office teams.** One person juggling dispatch, billing, and the phone will miss calls when two come in at once.
- **Owner-operators on the job.** If the owner is the phone line, every job means missed calls.

## Why don't customers just leave a voicemail?

**Because calling the next company is faster than waiting.** A homeowner with water on the floor or no heat doesn't know when you'll call back, so they keep dialing until someone answers.

Voicemail feels like a dead end. It gives no confirmation, no time estimate, and no next step. That's why the gap between your call and your response, known as [speed to lead](/glossary/speed-to-lead), often decides who gets the job. Whoever responds first with a clear answer has the edge.

## What are the hidden costs of missed calls?

**Beyond the lost job, missed calls waste your marketing spend, hand lifetime customers to competitors, and quietly hurt your reputation.** These costs don't show up on any report, which is why they're easy to ignore.

### Wasted marketing dollars

If you pay for Google Ads, Local Services Ads, lead platforms, or truck wraps, you pay to make the phone ring. A missed call means you paid for the lead and got nothing. Your cost per booked job goes up without anyone noticing.

### Lost lifetime value

A customer who books a competitor for an emergency often stays with them. They join that company's maintenance plan, call them for the replacement, and recommend them to neighbors.

### Reputation and reviews

Customers who couldn't reach you won't leave a review, but they'll remember. And every job you don't do is a review you don't get, which matters for local search and for how AI assistants recommend businesses. ## How do you find out how many calls you're missing?

**Pull the missed and unanswered call report from your phone system for the last 30 to 90 days, then filter out spam and known contacts.** Most VoIP and call-tracking systems have this report built in.

Steps:

1. Export all inbound calls with status (answered, missed, voicemail, abandoned).
2. Remove known spam, vendors, and internal numbers.
3. Note the day and time of each miss. Look for patterns: evenings, weekends, lunch hours, Monday mornings.
4. Check how many missed callers were called back, and how long that took.
5. Spot-check a few: did they end up booking?

The time-of-day pattern tells you which fix to prioritize. Mostly after hours? An AI receptionist is likely the best fit. Mostly during the day while your team is busy? Text-back plus overflow answering.

## How do you stop losing money to missed calls?

**Respond to every caller within seconds, even when no one can pick up.** The two highest-impact tools are missed-call text-back and an AI receptionist, ideally connected to your CRM and scheduling software.

| Fix | What it does | Best for | Effort to launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missed-call text-back | Auto-texts every missed caller in seconds and starts a conversation | Daytime overflow, any business | Low |
| AI receptionist | Answers calls, qualifies, books, and routes emergencies | After hours, peak season, small offices | Medium |
| Answering service | Live agents take messages | Businesses that want a human voice and can accept message-taking only | Low to medium |
| Additional staff | Dedicated CSR or dispatcher | Higher call volume, steady year-round | High |
| Callback process | Team calls back missed calls on a schedule | Better than nothing, but slow | Low |

### Missed-call text-back

[Missed-call text-back](/glossary/missed-call-text-back) is the simplest high-impact fix. When a call is missed, the caller gets a text like "Hi, this is Northside Heating and Air. Sorry we missed you. What can we help with today?" Many people will reply by text, which lets the AI ask qualifying questions and get them on the schedule.

### AI receptionist

An [AI receptionist](/services/ai-receptionist) answers the call itself, 24/7. It can collect the address and issue, check your service area, book into open slots, and transfer a true emergency to your on-call tech. If you're deciding between options, our comparison of [AI receptionists, answering services, and in-house staff](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service) breaks down the trade-offs.

### Follow-up that doesn't stop after one try

Some callers won't book on the first text. An automated [lead follow-up](/services/lead-follow-up) sequence keeps the conversation going for a few days, then stops as soon as they book or say no.

## How do missed calls differ by trade?

**The cost per missed call rises with average ticket size and urgency.** Emergency trades lose the job fastest; high-ticket trades lose the most per call.

- **[HVAC](/industries/hvac):** Heat waves and cold snaps create call spikes. A missed no-cool call can also be a missed system replacement.
- **[Plumbing](/industries/plumbing):** Emergencies like leaks and backups are extremely time-sensitive. Many customers won't wait.
- **[Electrical](/industries/electrical):** Mix of urgent and planned work. Missed quote calls for panels, EV chargers, and remodels carry high ticket values.
- **[Roofing](/industries/roofing):** Storm events create sudden lead surges, and each lead can be a large job.
- **Pest control:** Recurring service plans mean each lost customer is lost recurring revenue.
