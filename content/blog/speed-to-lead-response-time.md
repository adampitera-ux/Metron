When someone fills out your quote form or calls your business, the clock starts. In many industries, especially home services, that person has also contacted two or three of your competitors. The business that responds first and makes it easy to move forward often wins, regardless of who's cheapest. This guide covers what the research says and how to fix slow response times without hiring more staff.

## How fast should you respond to a new lead?

**Respond within minutes if you can, and within an hour at most.** In a study published in Harvard Business Review in 2011, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," researchers found that companies that tried to contact leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as companies that waited even an hour longer.

The same researchers tested 2,241 US companies by submitting web leads and timing the replies:

| Response time | Share of companies |
|---|---|
| Within an hour | 37% |
| 1 to 24 hours | 16% |
| More than 24 hours | 24% |
| Never responded | 23% |

That study is over a decade old, but the lesson has held up: most businesses respond slowly, and the ones that respond fast stand out.

## Why does speed matter so much?

**Speed matters because interest fades quickly and customers often contact several businesses at once.** The first business to respond has the best chance of starting the conversation, answering questions and booking the job.

It's especially true when:

- **The need is urgent,** such as a leak, a lockout, no heat or a storm-damaged tree.
- **Customers shop around,** like roofing, flooring and solar quotes.
- **Leads come from ads,** where you're paying for every click.
- **Leads arrive after hours,** when nobody's there to answer.

## Why do most small businesses respond slowly?

**Most small businesses respond slowly because the people who would respond are busy doing the actual work.** It's not a lack of effort.

- Owners and techs are on job sites.
- Front-desk staff are helping customers in person.
- Leads arrive at night and on weekends.
- Leads come through several channels: calls, forms, texts, Facebook, Google.
- There's no system that alerts the right person instantly.

## How can you respond to every lead in under a minute?

**Automate the first response, make it useful, and route qualified leads to a person fast.** Here's the setup that works for most small businesses:

1. **Missed-call text-back.** When you miss a call, the caller immediately gets a text from your business asking how you can help. See [what missed calls cost](/blog/missed-calls-cost-home-service-businesses).
2. **Instant form replies.** Web form leads get a personal-feeling text and email within seconds, not a generic autoresponder.
3. **AI conversation.** An AI assistant answers common questions, collects details and photos, and books an appointment or estimate on your calendar.
4. **Instant alerts.** Qualified leads trigger an alert to the right person with a clean summary.
5. **Follow-up sequence.** If the lead doesn't respond, timed follow-ups keep the conversation going without being pushy. See our [quote follow-up system](/blog/ai-quote-follow-up-system).

Make sure any automated texting follows the rules. Our [TCPA texting guide](/blog/tcpa-texting-rules-for-small-business) covers the basics.

## How do you measure your speed to lead?

**Test your own business like a customer would, then track response time in your CRM.**

- Submit your own website form at 8pm on a Friday and time the reply.
- Call your main number during a busy afternoon and after hours.
- Send a text and a Facebook message.
- Check how long real leads waited last month in your CRM or inbox.
