Texting is one of the best ways to reach customers. Open rates are high, replies are fast, and it's how most people prefer to confirm appointments. It's also one of the most regulated ways to contact people. If you're adding AI follow-up, missed-call text-back or automated reminders, you need to know the basic rules. This guide covers them in plain English.

**This is general information, not legal advice.** Rules change, and they depend on your industry and state. Use it to ask good questions, and talk to an attorney about your specific setup.

## What is the TCPA and why does it matter for AI texting?

**The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) is the federal law that governs automated calls and texts, and it applies to AI-powered follow-up just like any other automated messaging.** Violations can carry statutory damages per message, which is why class-action lawsuits over texting are common.

The TCPA cares about three things:

1. **What kind of message it is:** marketing versus informational, such as an appointment reminder or a reply to a request.
2. **How it's sent:** automated systems, prerecorded messages and artificial voices get stricter treatment.
3. **What consent you have:** whether the person agreed to receive it, and whether they've since told you to stop.

## Is replying to a customer different from marketing to them?

**Yes. A reply to someone who just contacted you about their own request is treated very differently from a promotional message you send unprompted.** This distinction is why missed-call text-back and lead follow-up can usually be set up safely.

Lower-risk messages usually look like:

- A text reply to a customer who just called and couldn't get through.
- A response to a quote request they submitted.
- Appointment confirmations and reminders for a visit they booked.
- Receipts, invoices and job updates.

Higher-risk messages usually look like:

- Promotions, discounts and seasonal offers.
- Reactivation campaigns to past customers.
- Texts to purchased lead lists.

For promotional messages, get clear, documented consent, for example a checkbox on your booking form that explains they'll receive marketing texts, with message frequency and how to opt out.

## How do opt-outs work now?

**Since April 11, 2025, consumers can revoke consent by any reasonable means, and businesses must honor revocation requests within 10 business days.** Words like STOP, QUIT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE, END and OPT OUT are treated as valid, and so are plain-language requests like "please stop texting me" or "remove me."

That last part matters for AI. Your system needs to recognize a natural-language opt-out, not just the keyword STOP, and immediately stop messaging that person.

The FCC has also been working on a broader rule about whether an opt-out from one type of message applies to all messages from your business. In January 2026 it delayed that requirement to January 31, 2027, and in September 2026 it proposed revising it. Expect more changes, and build your system so honoring an opt-out is automatic and immediate.

## Do AI voice calls count under the TCPA?

**Yes. In February 2024, the FCC unanimously ruled that calls made with AI-generated voices are 'artificial' voice calls under the TCPA.** Outbound AI voice calls need the same consent as prerecorded calls.

Inbound is different. When a customer calls your business and an AI receptionist answers, they called you. That's a normal business conversation, not a robocall. The rules bite when your AI calls people who didn't ask for it.

## What is 10DLC registration?

**10DLC is the US carrier system for business texting from standard 10-digit local numbers, and it requires you to register your business and how you'll use texting.** Unregistered business texts are likely to be filtered or blocked by carriers.

Registration typically includes:

- Your business name, EIN and website.
- Your use case, such as customer care, appointment reminders or marketing.
- Sample messages and how customers opt in.

Most texting platforms walk you through it. Your website should also have a clear privacy policy explaining how you use phone numbers.

## What else should every texting program include?

**Identify your business, respect quiet hours, keep records of consent, and make opting out easy.** A simple checklist:

- **Identify yourself** in the first message: "Hi, this is Smith Plumbing."
- **Respect quiet hours.** Avoid sending marketing texts early in the morning or late at night in the customer's time zone. Many businesses stick to roughly 8am to 9pm, and some states set their own limits.
- **Keep consent records:** when, where and how each person opted in.
- **Include opt-out instructions** in marketing messages and honor them right away.
- **Don't text purchased lists** without proper consent.
- **Check your state's rules.** Several states have their own telemarketing and texting laws that go further than federal law.
- **Announce call recording** when your state requires every party's consent.
