If you've ever lost a lead because it was buried in someone's inbox, or forgot to follow up on a quote, you've felt the problem a CRM solves. This guide explains what a CRM is, whether you need one and how to pick one without overpaying for features you'll never use.

## What is a CRM?

**A CRM (customer relationship management system) is software that keeps every customer, lead, conversation and follow-up in one place.** Instead of contact details scattered across phones, email and sticky notes, everyone on your team sees the same history and knows what to do next.

A typical CRM stores:

- **Contacts:** names, phone numbers, emails and addresses.
- **Leads and deals:** who's interested, in what, and at what stage.
- **Conversations:** calls, emails and texts tied to each customer.
- **Tasks and follow-ups:** reminders so nothing gets forgotten.
- **Reports:** where leads come from and how many turn into customers.

## Does your small business need a CRM?

**You probably need a CRM if leads come from more than one place, more than one person talks to customers, or follow-ups regularly slip.** Common signs:

- Leads arrive by phone, web form, Facebook and Google, and nobody tracks them all.
- You can't quickly answer "how many leads did we get last month, and from where?"
- Quotes go out and nobody follows up.
- A key employee leaves and their customer notes leave with them.
- You re-type customer details into several tools.

If you're a solo operator with a handful of repeat clients, a simple tool may be enough for now.

## CRM vs. field-service software: which do you need?

**Many home service and trade businesses don't need a separate CRM, because field-service software already includes CRM features.**

| Tool type | Best for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| General CRM | Sales-driven businesses, agencies, real estate, insurance, B2B | HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce, Zoho |
| Field-service software (includes basic CRM) | HVAC, plumbing, cleaning, landscaping, trades | Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan |
| Industry-specific platforms | Clinics, salons, gyms, law firms | Practice management and booking systems |

Before buying a new CRM, check what your current software already does.

## How much does a CRM cost?

**Many CRMs have free or low-cost starter plans, and paid plans are usually priced per user per month.** The bigger cost is often setup: importing contacts, building your pipeline, connecting your phone and email, and training your team. Prices change often, so compare current plans directly.

## How do you choose the right CRM?

**Choose the simplest CRM your team will actually use, that connects to the tools you already have.**

1. **List your must-haves.** For example: track leads from every source, automatic follow-up texts, a mobile app.
2. **Check integrations.** It should connect to your phone system, email, calendar, website forms and accounting.
3. **Test it with your team.** If people won't open it, it won't work.
4. **Start small.** Get leads and follow-ups working first, then add more.
5. **Plan the data move.** Clean up your contact list before importing.

## How do you get value from a CRM fast?

**Connect it to where leads come in, automate the first response, and set up a simple follow-up routine.** The biggest wins usually are:

- Every web form, call and message automatically creates a lead.
- New leads get an instant reply. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-response-time).
- Quotes trigger an automatic follow-up sequence. See the [AI quote follow-up system](/blog/ai-quote-follow-up-system).
- Weekly reports show where your best customers come from.

## The bottom line

A CRM is only as good as the habits around it. Keep it simple, connect it to your phones and website, and automate the follow-ups people forget.
