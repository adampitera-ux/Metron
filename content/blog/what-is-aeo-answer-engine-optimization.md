A growing number of your customers skip the list of search results. They ask a question, and a machine answers it. Sometimes that answer comes from Google's AI Overviews at the top of the page. Sometimes it comes from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Siri or Alexa. Either way, only a few businesses get named, and everyone else is easy to miss.

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is how you make sure your business is one of the ones that gets named. This guide explains what AEO is, how it works, and exactly what a small business should do about it.

## What is AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)?

**AEO is the practice of making your website easy for AI-powered "answer engines" to understand, trust and quote.** Instead of optimizing only to rank as a link, you optimize to become the direct answer, or a cited source inside that answer.

An "answer engine" is any tool that responds to a question with an answer rather than a list of pages. That includes:

- **Google AI Overviews** and featured snippets (see our [AI Overviews glossary entry](/glossary/ai-overviews))
- **ChatGPT** with search enabled
- **Perplexity**
- **Microsoft Copilot** and **Gemini**
- **Voice assistants** like Siri, Alexa and Google Assistant

For a deeper definition, see our [AEO glossary entry](/glossary/aeo).

## How is AEO different from SEO?

**SEO aims to rank your page in a list of results; AEO aims to get your content selected and quoted as the answer.** They overlap heavily, but the "win" looks different.

| | Traditional SEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank high in the list of links | Be the answer or a cited source |
| Unit of success | A click to your site | A mention, citation or quote (sometimes a click) |
| Content style | Long pages targeting keywords | Clear, direct answers to specific questions |
| Key technical signal | Crawlability, links, speed | All of SEO, plus structured data and clean, quotable structure |
| Where it shows up | Google and Bing results pages | AI Overviews, chat assistants, voice answers, snippets |

The important point: **AEO does not replace SEO.** Answer engines still lean on pages that are crawlable, trustworthy and well-organized. If your site is slow, thin or broken, AEO tactics won't save it. For the full comparison, read [SEO vs AEO vs GEO](/blog/seo-vs-aeo-vs-geo).

## Why does AEO matter for small businesses?

**Because the questions your customers ask are increasingly answered before they ever click, and the businesses named in those answers get the call.** If an AI tool can't find a clear answer on your site, it will use someone else's.

Think about the questions that come into your business every week:

- "How much does it cost to replace a water heater?"
- "Is a cracked tooth an emergency?"
- "Do I need a lawyer for a minor car accident?"
- "Why is my AC running but not cooling?"

Those are exactly the questions people now type or speak into AI tools. A plumbing company that has a clear, honest page answering "how much does water heater replacement cost in [city]" gives an answer engine something to quote. A plumbing company whose site just says "Quality Service Since 1998" gives it nothing.

Small businesses actually have an advantage here. You know your customers' questions better than any national content farm. You answer them on the phone every day. AEO is mostly about writing those answers down in a way machines can read.

## How does AEO work?

**Answer engines find pages that look relevant, judge whether they're trustworthy, then extract the clearest passage that answers the question.** Your job is to make each of those three steps easy.

No AI company publishes its full source-selection logic, so be skeptical of anyone who claims to know the exact formula. But the general pattern is well understood:

### 1. Retrieval: can the engine find you?

Many answer engines start by searching an index of web pages, much like a regular search engine. If your page isn't indexed, isn't crawlable, or is hidden behind scripts that don't render, it can't be retrieved. This is where basic technical SEO matters.

### 2. Evaluation: does the engine trust you?

Engines tend to favor sources that look credible: consistent business information, real reviews, clear authorship, and corroboration from other sites (directories, local news, industry associations). This is closely related to [entity SEO](/glossary/entity-seo), which is about making your business a clearly defined "thing" that machines recognize.

### 3. Extraction: can the engine quote you?

Finally, the engine needs a passage it can lift cleanly. A short, direct answer right under a clear question heading is far easier to extract than a key fact buried in paragraph six of a sales pitch.

## What does AEO-friendly content look like?

**AEO-friendly content puts the question in a heading and the answer in the first sentence or two beneath it, then adds detail.** It reads like a helpful expert answering a customer, not like an ad.

Here's a quick before-and-after for an HVAC company:

**Before:**
> At Smith Heating & Air, we pride ourselves on decades of excellence. Our NATE-certified technicians are committed to your comfort. Call today for all your heating needs!

**After:**
> ## How often should I service my furnace?
> Most manufacturers recommend servicing a gas furnace once a year, ideally in early fall before heavy use. An annual tune-up typically includes checking the heat exchanger, cleaning the burners, testing safety controls and replacing the filter.

The second version answers a real question. It's specific. It can be quoted on its own without any surrounding context. That's the whole game.

### Rules of thumb for answer-first writing

1. **Use the customer's words in your headings.** "How much does a root canal cost?" beats "Endodontic Pricing Information."
2. **Answer in the first 1–3 sentences.** Then explain, qualify and add detail.
3. **Make each answer self-contained.** An AI tool may quote one paragraph without the rest of the page.
4. **Be specific.** Name your city, your service area, typical timeframes and honest price ranges where you can.
5. **Use lists and tables** for steps, comparisons and options. They're easy for both people and machines to scan.
6. **Keep it honest.** Overpromising or vague claims are less likely to be quoted and more likely to lose you trust.

## What are the technical parts of AEO?

**The technical side of AEO is about removing guesswork: structured data, crawlable pages, and consistent business facts.** None of it is glamorous, but it's where many small business sites fall short.

| Technical element | What it does | Priority for most small businesses |
|---|---|---|
| [Schema markup](/glossary/schema-markup) (JSON-LD) | Labels your business, services and FAQs in a machine-readable format | High |
| Google Business Profile | Supplies core facts (hours, location, categories) to Google's systems | High |
| Consistent name, address, phone (NAP) | Helps engines confirm you're one real, trustworthy entity | High |
| Fast, mobile-friendly, crawlable pages | Lets engines retrieve and render your content | High |
| Clear heading structure (H2/H3) | Makes it easy to match questions to answers | Medium-high |
| [llms.txt](/glossary/llms-txt) file | An emerging, proposed convention to point AI tools to your key pages; not an official standard | Low-medium (cheap to add, unproven) |

We walk through each of these step by step in our [llms.txt, schema markup and technical AEO checklist](/blog/llms-txt-schema-markup-for-small-business).

## How do I start with AEO this month?

**Start by collecting the questions customers already ask you, answer the top ones clearly on your site, and fix your core business data.** You can make real progress in a few focused hours a week.

Here's a practical 30-day starting plan:

1. **Week 1: Gather questions.** Ask your front desk, techs or intake staff to write down every question customers ask for a week. Check your email, reviews and "People also ask" boxes in Google for more.
2. **Week 1: Test yourself.** Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google a handful of those questions, including "best [your service] in [your city]." Note who gets mentioned.
3. **Week 2: Fix your foundations.** Make sure your Google Business Profile is complete and accurate, and your name, address and phone match across your site and major directories.
4. **Week 2: Add an FAQ section** to your key service pages, using answer-first formatting.
5. **Week 3: Add schema markup.** At minimum, LocalBusiness (or a more specific type like Plumber, Dentist or LegalService) and FAQPage where you have genuine FAQs.
6. **Week 4: Write one strong answer page** for your most common high-value question, such as cost, timing or "do I need this."
7. **Ongoing: Build reviews.** Fresh, detailed reviews are a trust signal for people and machines. [Review automation](/services/review-automation) makes this consistent.

If your site is outdated or hard to edit, that's often the real bottleneck. A [website refresh](/services/website-refresh) built with clean structure and schema from day one can make every step above easier.

## What AEO mistakes should small businesses avoid?

**The biggest mistakes are chasing tricks, publishing generic AI-written filler, and ignoring the basics.** Answer engines reward clarity and credibility, not volume.

- **Mass-producing thin pages.** Fifty near-identical "plumber in [town]" pages with no real information rarely help and can hurt.
- **Marking up content that isn't on the page.** Schema should describe what visitors can actually see. Fake reviews or FAQs in schema violate search guidelines.
- **Hiding answers in images or PDFs.** If the price range lives only inside a JPEG flyer, machines may not read it.
- **Inconsistent business details.** An old phone number on a directory can create doubt about which information is correct.
- **Expecting guarantees.** Nobody can promise placement in an AI answer. Be wary of anyone who does.

## How does AEO fit with GEO?

**AEO focuses on being the direct answer; GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) focuses on being mentioned and recommended inside AI-generated responses.** In practice, the work overlaps by a lot.

If AEO is "make my page quotable," GEO is "make my business the one AI tools recommend." Our guide to [what GEO is and how it differs from SEO](/blog/what-is-geo-generative-engine-optimization) goes deeper, and you can see the [GEO glossary entry](/glossary/geo) for a quick definition. ## Key takeaways

- **AEO means optimizing to be the answer**, not just a link, across Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and voice assistants.
- **AEO builds on SEO.** Crawlable, fast, trustworthy pages are still the foundation.
- **Answer-first writing is the core skill:** question as a heading, direct answer in 1–3 sentences, then detail.
- **Technical basics matter:** schema markup, a complete Google Business Profile and consistent business details.
- **Small businesses have an edge** because you already know your customers' real questions.
- **No one can guarantee AI citations.** Focus on clarity, accuracy and trust, and measure over time.
